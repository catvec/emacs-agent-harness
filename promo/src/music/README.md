# The score

Original music and sound for the promo film, generated by code in this directory:
**no samples, no third-party audio, no network; part of the harness, GPL-3.0-or-later.** 48 kHz stereo
16-bit, 120 BPM, A minor, one chord a bar, exactly as long as the film
([`../timeline.ts`](../timeline.ts)'s `DURATION`: 18 bars = 1080 frames = 36.000 s for the
current footage).

```sh
cd promo
npm run music              # about 20 s -> build/score.wav + the QA's artefacts in build/ (exit 1 if a check fails)
npx tsx src/music/qa.ts    # rebuild from the timeline, compare with build/score.wav, run every check (exit 1 on failure)
```

The score reads the film's clock, the cue sheet of the footage. `timeline.ts` reads the
recorder's log (`build/footage/footage.json`, made by `promo/capture/capture.sh`). When that
log is not there, `footage-source.ts` scores against the recorded fixture
`footage-fixture.json` instead and says so; `PROMO_FOOTAGE_LOG=<file>` names another log and
always wins. **Record the footage and run `npm run music` again before rendering the finished
film.** `SCORE_QA_WARN_ONLY=1` makes `npm run music` exit 0 even when a check fails (the
checks are printed either way).

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `footage-source.ts` | Which footage log the timeline is read from (import it first). |
| `render-music.ts` | The entry point: build the score, write the WAV, run the QA. |
| `score.ts` | `buildScore(timeline)`: compose, mix, master, level, dither, encode. Pure: no file is touched, so the QA can build it again and compare bytes. Holds the mix (`MIX`). |
| `compose.ts` | `compose(timeline)`: the arrangement. A function of the timeline's exports (`SECTIONS`, `ENERGY`, `CUES`, `FALL`, `TENSION`, ...) and nothing else; no frame position is written in it. |
| `harmony.ts` | The data: chords, voicings, arp and bass patterns, the hook, the climb, the pickup. |
| `instruments.ts` | The music's voices: kick, clap, hats, snare, bass, pluck, pad, lead, big stab, crash, music-box ping, bell. Band-limited (PolyBLEP) oscillators, seeded noise, envelopes at both ends of every note. |
| `sfx.ts` | The sounds of the cues: typing tick, C-c C-c thock, click, send pop and whoosh, airy callout ping, FM chime, the fall (launch tick, downward whoosh), risers, the tuned sub drop, the thump. |
| `session.ts` | The mixing session: 21 named buses, reverb and delay sends, the sidechain duck, the gap mutes, the clean-room flush of reverb and delay at a gap, the limiter of everything-but-the-hits, per-bus filters and saturation. |
| `dsp.ts`, `master.ts` | Filters (TPT state-variable, RBJ biquads), an FDN reverb, a ping-pong delay; BS.1770 loudness, a 4x true-peak estimate, a lookahead limiter, a glue compressor, a mid/side widener. |
| `qa.ts` | The build listens to itself (below). |

## The form

Every row is a rule on the timeline, not a bar number: the table is what the current film
(plain bars 0-5, the board bars 6-15, the outro bars 16-17) comes out as.

| Bars | Time | What plays |
|---|---|---|
| 0-1 | 0-4 s | **Plain.** A warm detuned-saw pad swells in from nothing (its level and filter follow `ENERGY`), a few music-box notes, nothing else: the typing is the foreground. The first bar is 15 LU under the groove's median bar. |
| 2-4 | 4-10 s | The person has sent (frame 132). The next beat after the `sent` mark the music answers: a rising C major figure on the music box, and a soft sub heartbeat on every beat that grows. |
| 5 | 10-12 s | **Tension**, from `FALL + TENSION` frames before the landing (frame 300), no later than the bar before it: a noise riser and a saw riser climb, a snare roll goes eighths, sixteenths, thirty-seconds, sixteenth hats crescendo, the heartbeat swells. A frame and a half before the fall the risers stop (the launch lands in a hole); at the `fall` cue a downward whoosh, a reversed cymbal and an "inhale" run into **three frames of digital silence** (the reverb and the delay are emptied there, so nothing of the build comes back out of them). |
| 6 | 12-14 s | **The landing**, on the `impact` cue (frame 360): big kick, a sine that falls from A2 to A0, a crash, a thump of air and a wide A minor stab, saturated together and limited hard: the loudest moment of the film (K-weighted 100 ms: -8.0 LUFS against -10.2 for the loudest 100 ms elsewhere, and 0.52 dB above every other peak). The groove comes in on the next beat: four on the floor, claps on 2 and 4, eighth hats, offbeat bass. The typing of the first task happens here, so there is no arp yet. |
| 7-14 | 14-30 s | **The groove under the footage.** Chords `Am F C G Am F C G F G` (the loop `Am F C G`; the last two bars before the outro are `F G`). Sixteenth arp (carved down while a chime rings), open hats on the offbeats and sixteenth hats from the third bar, the gallop bass from the third bar, a pad that pumps under the kick. The two-bar hook (`G G E D E G / D B G A B` over C - G) in bars 8-9. A phrase is played only where no chime of the cues rings inside it: the closing climb (F - G, two bars) has no room (chimes at frames 761, 845 and 885), so the chimes are the melody there; the three-note pickup (D B E) plays in the second half of bar 15. |
| 15 | 30-32 s | The last bar: the pickup, a snare run on the last beat and a reversed cymbal into the bar line. |
| 16-17 | 32-36 s | **Outro.** The groove stops on the bar line (the stems of kick, bass, hats, clap, arp and lead are silent after the last kick has rung out). One last bright hit, A major with the ninth: kick, a stab, a crash, a ringing bell and its shimmer (C#6 E6 A6 B6 C#7), a pad that blooms; delay and reverb are the tail; the last 0.9 s close like a door; the last sample is exactly zero. |

More modules are more of the same: each `fall`/`impact` pair gets its own tension, gap and
landing; each module section its own groove, hook and cue sounds; a module that follows a module
gives up its last bar to the next build. QA builds a synthetic film with a plain act, two
modules and an outro and checks it too.

## How every cue is answered

Musical events sit on the grid, in samples: `(bar * 16 + step) * 6000`. The sounds of cues sit
on their own frame, `frame * 1600` samples, wherever the grid is; only a key press may move
(onto the sixteenth beside it, and only when that is less than 0.9 frame away).
Pitched sounds take their notes from the chord of the bar they fall in, so the cues are always
in key; sounds that happen on one frame together are made a little quieter.

| Cue (in the current footage) | Sound | Bus |
|---|---|---|
| `type` (103) | A soft "tok": a band-passed noise click, a duller body and a short tuned thock whose pitch is picked from the chord's pentatonic (about 165-350 Hz), a little velocity, pan and size variation per key (seeded per cue, so changing one never changes another). **Thinned** to at most 12 a second and no two closer than 2 frames (75 of 103 kept); louder with the bar's `ENERGY` (up to +14 dB, the groove gets in its way); **ducked** from 2 frames before the fall to 4 after the landing. | `type` |
| `key` C-c C-c (4) | A bigger thock (F3-C4, by chord), the click of the plastic, a second tap as the key bottoms out 34 ms later, a faint key-up. | `key` |
| `mark` `sent`, `submit-N` (4) | On the key's frame: a round "pop" (a sine falling 570 to 190 Hz), then a third of a second of air that rises from 700 Hz to 6.5 kHz as it pans left to right. | `send` |
| `click` (4) | A crisp tick, a bright blip on a chord tone around 1.3-2.1 kHz, a faint press under it. | `click` |
| `callout` (4) | A small airy ping: a sine that glides up a fifth into a chord tone, a breath of noise around it, detuned left and right. The arp and the lead step back 3 dB while it rings. | `callout` |
| `highlight` (1) | A bright FM chime (a harmonic modulator: its overtones are the note's own harmonics) on a chord tone. | `chime` |
| `mark` `review-N` (1) | Two chimes, a fourth apart, 85 ms apart: the chord's fifth, then its root an octave up. The arp, lead and pad step back while they ring. | `chime` |
| `mark` `verify-N` (2) | The resolve: the chord's triad rises through an octave to its root (55 ms a note) and rings, over a low root and a high sparkle. The chord of the bar decides the notes (F A C F, then G B D G). | `chime` |
| `fall` (1) | A launch tick and a blip that falls from 2.7 kHz (so the start has an onset), air that sweeps down from 7 kHz to 280 Hz while it swells, a faint tone falling A5 to A2, a reversed cymbal and a reversed boom that rises to A2: all end in the gap. | `fall` |
| `impact` (1) | Big kick + A sub drop + crash + thump + wide Am stab (on the `impact` bus, which passes a saturator and bypasses the limiter of everything else), after the three-frame gap. | `impact` |

## Mix and master

Reverb and delay are sends (a dotted-eighth ping-pong delay, an 8-line FDN reverb ducked by the kick). The
music steps out of 2-6 kHz, where the typing's clicks live (a broad -4 dB cut on the buses that
play there). Everything except the hits passes a bus limiter that holds it 8.1 dB above its own
loudness, so the groove, the typing and the chimes can never reach the ceiling: only the hits do.
Then the master: EQ (28 Hz high-pass, an 8th-order low-pass at 13.5 kHz), mid/side widening
above 300 Hz (bass and kick stay centred and mono), a glue compressor, the gap, the closing fade,
the level search (the gain that puts the integrated loudness on -14 LUFS with a lookahead limiter
holding the true peak under -1.1 dBTP), TPDF dither (seeded), 16 bits.

## What the build checks

`npm run music` and `npx tsx src/music/qa.ts` run 51 checks, all of which must pass; the
numbers go to `build/score-report.json` (every cue's measured onset in `sync.stemRows`), the
table of what each cue of the timeline was answered with to `build/score-cues.json`, and the
pictures to `build/score-spectrogram.png` and `build/score-waveform.png`.
Numbers for the footage log (the fixture is a copy of it; sha256 of `score.wav`:
`14b3a22b54ae99d66a1272381887ef38d461a335b6346291ab7bbf384a90de96`):

- **Format and level**: 48 kHz, 2 channels, 16 bit, exactly `DURATION * 1600` samples; integrated
  loudness -14.0 LUFS (own BS.1770 and ffmpeg `ebur128` agree); true peak -1.25 dBTP (ffmpeg -1.2);
  sample peak -1.35 dBFS, no full-scale sample; no DC; no NaN; stereo correlation 0.83;
  the limiter takes at most 6.9 dB, at the landing, and next to nothing in the groove.
- **Deterministic**: the score is built twice in the QA and compared with the file on disk; two
  separate processes write the same bytes.
- **Shape**: bar loudness follows `ENERGY` (Spearman 0.82); bar 0 is -28.2 LUFS against -12.8 for
  the groove's median bar; the loudness of the thirds of the build rises -19.6, -13.4, -12.9 LUFS; the gap is
  digital silence (-99.2 dBFS, 86.6 dB under the build); the landing is the loudest 100 ms (K-weighted,
  2.2 LU over any other), the loudest 400 ms and the highest peak (by 0.52 dB); the outro hit is 2.4 LU
  under it; the groove stops on the bar line; the file ends on an exact zero.
- **Sync**: for every cue, an onset on the instrument that plays it within one frame (33 ms) of the
  cue's frame, measured on the bus's own stem by onset detection and refined to the sample:
  `type` 75/75 (median 0.4 ms, max 0.5), `key` 4/4 (max 8.7 ms: the ones nudged onto the grid),
  `mark:sent|submit` 4/4 (max 24.7 ms), `mark:review` 1/1, `mark:verify` 2/2 (max 0.3), `click` 4/4,
  `callout` 4/4 (airy, 1.3 ms), `highlight` 1/1 (0.3 ms), `fall` 1/1 (0.3 ms), `impact` 1/1 (0.4 ms). In the
  final mix, the impact, the fall, the key presses, the sends and the chimes each have a fresh
  onset within a frame, 6 dB above what came before for the impact and the fall (85.9 and 17.5 dB) and
  4 dB for the rest (5.5 to 40.2 dB); the expected cue train correlates with the audio at lag 0.0 ms.
- **The typing**: at most 12 ticks in any second, none closer than 2 frames; in the 2-6 kHz band a
  tick's first 8 ms is a median 24.3 dB over the music alone in the plain bars and 3.1 dB in the
  groove (10th percentile 1.3 dB); the ticks are 10.2 to 11.9 LU under the music over the typing runs
  in the groove.
- **Grid**: the onset autocorrelation of the groove peaks at 500.0 ms (a beat) and 2000.0 ms (a
  bar), on-grid / off-grid = 3.08.
- **Spectrum**: 61% of the groove's energy below 250 Hz, 4.0% at 2-10 kHz, 2.3% at 2-5 kHz,
  nothing above 17 kHz but the dither (-105 dBFS), the content above 250 Hz wide (correlation
  0.62) yet mono-safe, crest factor of the groove 13.4 dB.
- **Tune**: 94.8% of the tonal energy is in A natural minor, the tonic triad is all there, the outro
  adds C#; every hook note is within 12 cents of the intended pitch (harmonic-sum estimate).
- **Hygiene**: every one of the 637 placed events starts and ends within 3% of zero. (Ad hoc, not in the
  QA: a scan of the final file for sudden jumps finds none that is not an intended onset or a delay echo of one.)
- **The score is a function of the timeline**: a synthetic film (plain, two modules, an outro, 14
  bars) is built too: both impacts on their frame, -14.01 LUFS, -1.10 dBTP, whole-file -14.5 LUFS, no clicks. (Ad hoc, not
  in the QA: 16 random films of 6-20 bars, 0-3 modules, with and without plain act, outro or
  cues, all came out at -14.0 +/- 0.03 LUFS, true peak at or under -1.11 dBTP, no clicks, every impact
  on its frame.)

## What nobody has heard

The score was written and checked without anyone listening to it: everything above is a
measurement or a spectrogram, and the timbres (does the tick sound like typing, is the hit big,
is the hook catchy, are the chimes too sweet) are reasoning from the synthesis. The sound
effects were rendered one at a time and looked at as spectrograms; the low octaves of
`score-spectrogram.png` are coarse (ffmpeg's FFT is too short for them: a 27 Hz sine looks like
a smear), so the sub-bass was checked with a separate analysis instead.
A listen is worth the 36 seconds.
